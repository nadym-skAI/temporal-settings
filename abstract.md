We give three representations of finite loopless temporal graphs on a fixed vertex set: a sequence of snapshots, a footprint with finite time labels, and a chronological list of temporal edge occurrences. The horizon is explicit, including for an edgeless graph. Equal-time occurrences have no prescribed order. Both orientations of an undirected edge denote the same physical edge.

We define strict and nonstrict temporal walks, paths, reachability and temporal graph settings. Removing a closed segment turns a walk into a shorter walk with the same endpoints. On proper graphs, the departure times of every nonstrict path are already strictly increasing. Examples distinguish paths from walks, simplicity from properness, and directed from undirected properness.

An occurrence may have a natural traversal time. Waiting is allowed, the horizon bounds departure times, and the final arrival may follow the last snapshot. Uniform traversal times one and zero recover strict and nonstrict paths respectively, with the same vertices and departure times, and therefore the same reachability.

Related archive concepts include [strict paths in labelled multigraphs for gossiping](https://laxarchive.org/lax-630294/Lax630294.Gossiping.html) and [undirected snapshot graphs with timed visits](https://laxarchive.org/lax-623795/Lax623795.TemporalGraphs.html).

For a fixed source, target, release time and arrival deadline, we define shortest, fastest, foremost, latest-departure and minimum-waiting paths. Each objective admits an optimal path whenever the query is feasible.
